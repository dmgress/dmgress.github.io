In 2015 I spent several months using 4G mobile internet through a dongle. That's right, not just for my phone but actually for all my internet needs in a country and society accustomed to high speed flat fee internet. It taught me two things: 1) what and who I am, 2) what my world thinks is normal.

This rant has been lingering as a draft for over a year. There is a lot of frustration in it which I decided to keep in order to capture my state of mind at that the moment of writing. Reading it again made me relive and reflect on my past, which I consider a good thing.

What did I consume before the switch

I used the internet for video chat, online learning, streaming tv shows and music, backups, and getting lost in a spiral of memes.

Compared to my first internet experience, 56K modem on a landline disrupted by phone calls, this is excessive. Over time this kind of internet usage has become the norm and I have grown accustomed to it.

Making the switch to 4G

The reason I switched to 4G internet was a neighbour moving in, which caused some confusion with the wiring of the landline I use for DSL. Being a good neighbour, I tried to resolve it but some people prefer being selfish. I tried resolving it with customer support of the internet provider, they basically said: "it happens, nothing we can do."

After a few frustrating days I didn't see the harm of switching to 4G internet, the offer seemed good and the possibility of carrying around my own reliable wifi was appealing.

What I got with 4G internet

Actually two things:

  1. Sucky internet and lots of frustration because of it. I can't do streaming, updates or anything else unlimited. Constantly keeping in mind the fun only lasts as long as I didn't hit the cap.

  2. An understanding how ISPs in the Netherlands basically suck at customer service, not necessarily in training but also in attitude. To be fair, there are gems but most of them seem to be scripted dung heads with feathers up their ass and... well let's stay civil, their just running their script and you're just another number to them.

  3. Of course, it's possible to enjoy more high speed with top ups. So yeah, my bills grew during (mandatory) off days.

To compensate I started an exercise program that knocked me out during most of my free time, learned to cherish public free WiFi, and downloaded videos to watch at a later time.

What the internet thinks is normal

Browsers turned into powerful platforms with fast rendering engines, but the internet seems slower. What happened? Advertising and people tracking. That's what happened. Another thing which is kinda big, literally, is the initial page size. What does this do to a data cap? Yes, it blasts right through it if you're not careful.

The assumption seems to be the world needs optimal experience. This results in tailoring, for instance, streaming to the amount of bandwidth measured. Once I hit the data cap that went bad...

What I need to consume

To keep my edge and sanity I need:

  • To keep communications with friends and family
  • Be able to do online learning, which usually involves streaming video
  • Be able to update my systems or try out new stuff

In addition it's nice to have online backups of your (phone) data.confusion
